Orbital Mechanics & Re-entry

Orbital Adjustments

Quadratic equations calculate optimal thruster burns to adjust the ISS orbit. The equation models the parabolic path of orbital adjustments.

Δv = a·t² + b·t + c

Orbital Mechanics

To adjust the ISS orbit, thrusters fire for precise durations calculated using quadratic equations:

Δv = ½a·t² + v₀·t

Where:

  • Δv = Change in velocity (m/s)
  • a = Acceleration from thrusters (m/s²)
  • t = Burn duration (seconds)
  • v₀ = Initial relative velocity (m/s)

These calculations ensure the ISS maintains its precise orbital position at 424 km altitude.

Re-entry Physics

During re-entry, the capsule's descent follows a precisely calculated quadratic trajectory. The equation predicts landing location and peak heating points.

h(x) = a·x² + b·x + c

Re-entry Calculations

The capsule's re-entry path is modeled by a quadratic equation:

h(x) = - (g/(2vₓ²)) · x² + (vᵧ/vₓ) · x + h₀

Where:

  • h(x) = Altitude at horizontal distance x
  • g = Gravitational acceleration (m/s²)
  • vₓ = Horizontal velocity component (m/s)
  • vᵧ = Vertical velocity component (m/s)
  • h₀ = Initial altitude (m)

This equation ensured Group Captain Shukla's capsule landed within 1 km of the recovery ship.
