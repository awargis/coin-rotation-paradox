Proof & Verification

Step 1: The Setup

We construct three circles, using each side of $\triangle ABC$ as a diameter.
$$ \omega_a: \text{diameter } \overline{BC} \\ \omega_b: \text{diameter } \overline{CA} \\ \omega_c: \text{diameter } \overline{AB} $$

Step 2: The Orthocenter

The three altitudes (lines from a vertex perpendicular to the opposite side) intersect at a single point, $H$, the **orthocenter**. The feet of the altitudes are D, E, and F.

Step 3: The Radical Axis

The **radical axis** of two circles is the set of points having equal power to both. We claim each altitude is a radical axis. For any point $P$ on the altitude from $A$:
$$ \mathrm{Pow}(P, \omega_b) = \mathrm{Pow}(P, \omega_c) $$ Key Insight: The feet of the altitudes E and F lie on the circle $\omega_a$ (with diameter BC) because $\angle BEC$ and $\angle BFC$ are right angles subtended by the diameter.

Step 4: Conclusion

Since the three altitudes are the three radical axes, their common intersection point, $H$, must be the **radical center**.
$$ \text{Radical Center} = \text{Orthocenter} $$
