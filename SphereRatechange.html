📚 Problem Statement

If the volume of a spherical ball is increasing at the rate of 4π cc/sec, then what is the rate of increase of its radius (in cm/sec), when the volume is 288π cc?

V = 4⁄3πr³
1 Differentiate the volume formula with respect to time:
dV/dt = 4πr²(dr/dt)
2 Solve for the radius when V = 288π:
288π = 4⁄3πr³ → r³ = 216 → r = 6 cm
3 Plug in known values and solve for dr/dt:
4π = 4π(6)²(dr/dt) → dr/dt = 1/36 cm/sec
dr/dt = 1/36 ≈ 0.0278 cm/sec

Key Insight

Notice how the rate of change of the radius (dr/dt) depends on the current radius. Even though the volume is growing at a constant rate (dV/dt = 4π cc/sec), the radius grows more slowly as the sphere gets larger.

This is because the same amount of added volume spreads over a larger surface area as the sphere grows. At r = 6cm, each square centimeter of the sphere's surface only needs to expand by 0.0278 cm to accommodate the volume increase.
