Elliptical Reflection Simulator Help

About Ellipses

An ellipse is a closed curve where the sum of the distances from any point on the curve to the two foci (F₁ and F₂) is constant. This property leads to interesting reflection characteristics:

  • Any ray emitted from one focus will reflect to the other focus, regardless of the emission angle.
  • The constant sum of distances is equal to 2a, where 'a' is the semi-major axis.
  • The distance between foci is 2c, where c = √(a² - b²) and 'b' is the semi-minor axis.

Controls

  • Semi-major axis (a): Controls the longest radius of the ellipse.
  • Semi-minor axis (b): Controls the shortest radius of the ellipse.
  • Emission Angle: Sets the angle (in degrees) for emitted rays.
  • Max Bounces: Sets how many times a ray will bounce inside the ellipse.

Applications

The simulator demonstrates several real-world applications of elliptical reflection:

  • Whispering Galleries: Sound waves reflect between foci, allowing whispers to be heard clearly across large spaces.
  • Telescope Mirrors: Elliptical mirrors focus light from one focal point to another.
  • Stage Lighting: Elliptical reflectors concentrate light from a bulb at one focus to a stage area at the other.
  • Medical Devices: Elliptical reflectors are used in lithotripters to focus shock waves on kidney stones.
