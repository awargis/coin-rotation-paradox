🧮 Mathematical Solution

Since each room must have at least 1 person, and there are 6 people for 4 rooms, the only possible distribution is (2, 2, 1, 1).

Step 1: Choose 2 rooms to have 2 people each

C(4, 2) = 6 ways

Step 2: Select 2 people for the first double room

C(6, 2) = 15 ways

Step 3: Select 2 people for the second double room

C(4, 2) = 6 ways

Step 4: Distribute remaining 2 people to the two single rooms

2! = 2 ways

Total ways: 6 × 15 × 6 × 2 = 1080

This problem demonstrates how combinatorial principles can be applied to constrained distribution problems. The key insight is recognizing that the only valid distribution pattern is (2, 2, 1, 1).
