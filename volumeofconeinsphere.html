OPTIMIZATION REVEALED
A cylinder inscribed in a cone has maximum curved surface area when its diameter equals the cone's base radius
MATHEMATICAL INSIGHT

Consider a cone with height H and base radius R. A cylinder is inscribed with height h and radius r.

\text{Using similar triangles: } \frac{r}{R} = \frac{H - h}{H} \implies r = R \left(1 - \frac{h}{H}\right)

The curved surface area (CSA) of the cylinder is:

\text{CSA} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi R \left(1 - \frac{h}{H}\right) h

To maximize the CSA, we take the derivative with respect to h and set it to zero:

\frac{d}{dh} \left[ 2\pi R \left(h - \frac{h^2}{H}\right) \right] = 2\pi R \left(1 - \frac{2h}{H}\right) = 0

Solving gives the optimal height:

h = \frac{H}{2} \implies r = R \left(1 - \frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{R}{2}

Thus, the cylinder diameter is 2r = R, which equals the cone's base radius.
