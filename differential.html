What is a Differential?

A differential represents an infinitely small change in a variable. In calculus:

dy = f'(x) · dx

Where dx is a small change in the input, and dy is the corresponding change along the tangent line. This concept is fundamental for approximating changes in functions.

Differential vs. Actual Change

The actual change in the function (Δy) is:

Δy = f(x + dx) - f(x)

While the differential (dy) is the change predicted by the derivative. For small dx, dy ≈ Δy, but as dx increases, the approximation becomes less accurate. The difference illustrates the function's curvature.

Applications of Differentials

Differentials are essential for:

  • Linear approximations of complex functions
  • Error propagation in scientific measurements
  • Solving differential equations
  • Understanding rates of change in physics
  • Optimization problems in engineering
