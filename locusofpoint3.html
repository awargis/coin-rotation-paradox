Locus in Coordinate Geometry

An Ultimate Visualization By Aditya Wargis

What is a Locus?

Locus is a set of points that satisfy a given geometric condition.

In simpler terms, if you have a rule or condition, the locus is the path traced by all points that follow that rule.

Core Concepts

1. Circle as a locus: All points equidistant from a fixed point (center).

2. Parabola as a locus: All points equidistant from a fixed point (focus) and a fixed line (directrix).

3. Ellipse as a locus: All points where the sum of distances to two fixed points (foci) is constant.

4. Hyperbola as a locus: All points where the difference of distances to two fixed points (foci) is constant.

Mathematical Foundation

The concept of locus bridges geometry and algebra. By representing geometric conditions as algebraic equations, we can analyze and visualize complex relationships.

Example: Finding Locus

Find the locus of a point that moves so that its distance from point A(2,3) is twice its distance from point B(-1,1).

Solution: Let P(x,y) be the moving point. Then:

PA = 2PB ⇒ √[(x-2)²+(y-3)²] = 2√[(x+1)²+(y-1)²]

Squaring both sides and simplifying gives the equation of a circle.

(x-2)² + (y-3)² = 4[(x+1)² + (y-1)²]
x² - 4x + 4 + y² - 6y + 9 = 4x² + 8x + 4 + 4y² - 8y + 4
3x² + 12x + 3y² - 2y - 5 = 0
