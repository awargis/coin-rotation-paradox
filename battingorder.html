🏏 Cricket Batting Order Puzzle

Understanding the Problem

We have 11 players (A through K) who need to be arranged in a batting order.

There are two constraints:

  1. Player A must bat before Player J (A → J)
  2. Player J must bat after Player G (G → J)

All other players (B, C, D, E, F, H, I, K) can bat in any position.

We need to find how many valid batting orders satisfy these constraints.

Total permutations without constraints: 11! = 39,916,800

With constraints A before J and G before J:

Probability that A comes before J = 1/2

Probability that G comes before J = 1/2

But these events are not independent...

For three distinct items, there are 3! = 6 possible orders

Valid orders: A → G → J, G → A → J

So 2 out of 6 orders satisfy both constraints

Thus, valid orders = (2/6) × 11! = (1/3) × 39,916,800

Total valid batting orders: 13,305,600
