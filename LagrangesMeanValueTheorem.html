Slope Equality

\( f'(c) = 0.00 \)

📜 Theorem Statement

Conditions

  • The function \(f\) is continuous on the closed interval \([a, b]\)
  • The function \(f\) is differentiable on the open interval \((a, b)\)
\[ f'(c) = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a} \]

Conclusion

There exists at least one point \(c\) in \((a, b)\) such that the derivative at \(c\) equals the average rate of change over \([a, b]\).

💡 Geometric Interpretation

The Mean Value Theorem has a beautiful geometric interpretation that makes it intuitive to understand.

Imagine drawing a straight line (called a secant line) between two points on a curve: \(A(a, f(a))\) and \(B(b, f(b))\). The slope of this secant line is:

\[m_{AB} = \frac{f(b) - f(a)}{b - a}\]

According to the Mean Value Theorem, there must be at least one point \(P(c, f(c))\) on the curve between A and B where the tangent line to the curve is parallel to this secant line.

Key Insights

  • Secant Line: Connects endpoints A and B
  • Tangent Line: Touches the curve at point P
  • Parallel Lines: Both lines have identical slopes
  • Instantaneous = Average: At point P, the derivative equals the average rate of change
  • Existence: At least one such point exists when the conditions are met
